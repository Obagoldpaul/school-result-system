Teaching & Academics

Learn how to enter and manage student scores, process results, view class performance and prepare student report cards.

1. Entering Student Scores

Paul SchoolHub allows authorised teachers and school management users to enter students' Continuous Assessment (CA) and examination scores for their assigned classes and subjects.

Selecting a Class and Subject
  1. Open Score Entry from your school navigation menu.
  2. The Select Class & Subject to Enter Scores page will open.
  3. Use the filters to narrow down the allocations you want to view.
  4. You can filter by Class, Academic Session, Term, Subject and Status.
  5. Select Filter to apply your choices.
  6. Use Clear to remove the filters.
  7. Allocations are grouped according to their classes.

Teachers see allocations according to their teaching responsibilities. Class teachers can access allocations for their assigned classes, while management users can view allocations across their school.

Entering and Saving Scores
  1. Locate the required class, subject and term.
  2. Select Enter/View Scores.
  3. The student score-entry table will display the students assigned to that allocation.
  4. Enter each student's CA score in the CA column.
  5. Enter the examination score in the Exam column.
  6. Select Save All Scores to save the entries.
Score limits: Continuous Assessment (CA) has a maximum of 40 marks, while the examination has a maximum of 60 marks. Together, they make a total of 100 marks.
Important: Scores can only be edited while the allocation is in Draft status. Once submitted for review, the scores can no longer be edited through the normal score-entry process.

2. Understanding the Score Approval Process

Every subject allocation follows a controlled workflow before its results become available to students. The process helps ensure that scores are entered, reviewed and approved before publication.

The Five Allocation Statuses
  1. Draft: Scores are being entered or updated by the authorised teacher. The allocation can still be edited.
  2. Submitted: The teacher has submitted the scores for review. Further editing is no longer allowed.
  3. Reviewed: An authorised reviewer has checked the scores and may add a class teacher's comment.
  4. Approved: School management has approved the reviewed scores.
  5. Published: School management has published the approved results.
Submitting Scores for Review
  1. Return to the score allocation list.
  2. Locate the allocation with Draft status.
  3. Select Submit for Review.
  4. Confirm the browser prompt after checking the allocation.
Before submitting: Confirm that all students' CA and examination scores are correct. You will not be able to edit the scores after submission.
Reviewing Submitted Scores
  1. Locate an allocation with Submitted status.
  2. Select Review.
  3. Check the allocation details and scores.
  4. Enter a Class Teacher's Comment where necessary.
  5. Select Mark as Reviewed.

Reviewing is restricted to authorised users. Management users and the class teacher assigned to the allocation's class can review submitted allocations, subject to their permissions.

Approving Results
  1. Locate an allocation with Reviewed status.
  2. Select Approve.
  3. Complete the approval action.

Approval is restricted to authorised school management users.

Publishing Results
  1. Locate an allocation with Approved status.
  2. Select Publish.
  3. Confirm the publication prompt.

Publishing makes the approved allocation available for the school's result publication process. Only authorised management users can publish results.

Remember: The allocation must move through the correct sequence: Draft → Submitted → Reviewed → Approved → Published.

3. Viewing Class Results

The Class Results page allows authorised school users to view the performance of students in a selected class for a particular academic session and term.

Viewing Results
  1. Open Class Results.
  2. Select the required class.
  3. Select the academic session.
  4. Select the academic term belonging to that session.
  5. Select View Results.

The results page displays a class performance summary, including:

  • Number of students.
  • Highest total score.
  • Top average score.

The results table displays each student's position, full name, admission number, total score, average and a link to view the student's report card.

Class teacher access: Class teachers are restricted to results for their assigned classes. Other authorised staff can access classes within their own school according to their permissions.

The page also displays the term's publication status. This term-level status should be distinguished from the individual Draft, Submitted, Reviewed, Approved and Published statuses of subject allocations.

4. Viewing Student Report Cards

A student report card provides a summary of the student's academic performance and other assessment information for a selected term.

Opening a Report Card
  1. Open the relevant student's report card from the class results page, or use the available report-card selection page.
  2. Select the academic term you want to view.
  3. The report card will display the student's information and performance for that term.

The report card is restricted to the student's own school. Management users can view students in their school. Students can view only their own report cards, while class teachers can view students assigned to their class.

Information Displayed on the Report Card
  • School information: School name, logo, motto, address and contact details where configured.
  • Student information: Student name, admission number, class and passport photograph where available.
  • Academic information: Academic session, term, subjects, CA scores, examination scores, totals, averages, grades and remarks.
  • Performance summary: Total marks, current-term percentage, cumulative percentage and marks obtainable.
  • Class position: Displayed when the school has enabled the Show Class Position setting.
  • Social habits: Responsibility, Leadership, Hardworking and Neatness.
  • Attendance summary: Days school opened, days present, days absent and attendance percentage.
  • Remarks: Class teacher's comment and principal's remark.
  • Principal information: Principal's name and signature where configured.
Understanding the Grading Scale

The report card currently displays the following grading scale:

Grade Score Range
A175–100
B270–74
B365–69
C460–64
C555–59
C650–54
D745–49
E840–44
F90–39
Student access: Students can view their report cards only when all results for their class and the selected term have been fully published.

5. Editing Report Card Remarks and Social Habits

Authorised staff can update the additional information displayed on a student's report card.

Editing Report Details
  1. Open the student's report card.
  2. Select Edit Remarks/Attendance.
  3. Update the available fields.
  4. Select Save.

The form can contain the following fields:

  • Class teacher's remark.
  • Principal's remark.
  • Responsibility.
  • Leadership.
  • Hardworking.
  • Neatness.

The remark fields shown depend on the user's assigned permissions. The social-habit fields allow the school to record the student's assessment using grades A–F.

Who can edit? Management users can edit report details for students in their school. Class teachers can edit details only for students in their assigned classes. Specific permissions control access to teacher and principal remarks.
Editing a Teacher's Remark Separately

Users with the appropriate permission can also use the separate Teacher Remark page when that action is available.

  1. Open the Teacher Remark page for the student and term.
  2. Review the displayed student name and term.
  3. Enter or update the teacher's remark.
  4. Select Save Teacher Remark.

Select Cancel to return to the student's report card without saving.

Attendance figures: Attendance information displayed on the report card is calculated separately. The Edit Remarks/Attendance form does not directly edit the attendance figures.

6. Downloading Report Cards

Staff and students who have access to a report card can use the PDF option where the school's subscription includes Advanced Reporting.

  1. Open the student's report card.
  2. Select Download as PDF.
  3. The report card will be generated and downloaded as a PDF file.
Package availability: PDF report-card downloads require the Advanced Reporting feature. Access is subject to the school's active subscription and the user's report-card permissions.

7. Managing Subject Allocations

Subject allocations connect subjects, classes, academic terms and teachers. They determine which teacher is responsible for teaching and entering scores for a particular subject and class.

Viewing Subject Allocations
  1. Open Subject Allocations from the school navigation menu.
  2. Select an academic term from the Academic Term dropdown.
  3. Optionally select a class to narrow down the list.
  4. The allocations will be displayed, grouped according to school sections and classes.
  5. Review each subject, its assigned teacher and its current status.

Authorised staff can view allocations within their school. The available management actions depend on the user's permissions and role.

Creating an Individual Allocation

School management users can assign a teacher to a subject for a particular class and academic term.

  1. Open the Subject Allocations page.
  2. Select New Allocation.
  3. Select the teacher.
  4. Select the subject.
  5. Select the class.
  6. Select the academic term.
  7. Select Save Allocation.
Allocating Multiple Subjects at Once

The Bulk Subject Allocation feature allows management users to assign teachers to several subjects for one class and term without creating each allocation separately.

  1. Open Subject Allocations.
  2. Select Bulk Allocate Subjects.
  3. Choose the required class and academic term.
  4. Select Load Subjects.
  5. The active subjects assigned to that class will appear in a table.
  6. Choose a teacher from the dropdown beside each subject.
  7. Select Allocate All to save the assignments.

Existing allocations are displayed with their current teacher and status. Existing Draft allocations can have their assigned teacher updated. Allocations that have already moved beyond Draft are not overwritten.

Assign All and Clear All: Assign All fills empty teacher dropdowns using the first teacher selected in the table. Clear All removes the selected teachers from the dropdowns. These buttons only adjust the form selections; you must still select Allocate All to save the allocations.
Important: Subjects without a selected teacher are skipped when allocations are saved. Existing allocations that cannot be updated because they are already in the approval workflow are also skipped.
Carrying Allocations Forward

Instead of recreating subject and teacher assignments for every term, management users can carry allocations from an earlier term into another term.

  1. Select Carry Forward Allocations from the Subject Allocations page.
  2. Choose the source academic session and term.
  3. Choose the destination academic session and term.
  4. Review your selections.
  5. Select Carry Forward Allocations to complete the operation.

The system carries forward the subject, class and assigned teacher for each allocation.

Existing allocations are preserved: If the same subject is already allocated to the same class in the destination term, the system skips it instead of creating a duplicate or replacing the existing allocation. The system displays the number of allocations created and skipped.
Understanding Allocation Statuses

Each allocation displays its current status:

  • Draft: The allocation is still in its initial stage.
  • Submitted: Scores have been submitted for review.
  • Reviewed: The submitted scores have been reviewed.
  • Approved: The scores have been approved by management.
  • Published: The results have been published.

These statuses are part of the score approval process explained in the earlier sections of this guide.

Access: Viewing allocations requires the appropriate subject viewing permission. Creating individual, bulk and carry-forward allocations requires the subject assignment permission. Management action buttons are displayed according to the current interface.

8. Managing Student Attendance

Paul SchoolHub allows authorised school staff to record daily student attendance, monitor class attendance statistics and allow students to view their own attendance history.

Selecting a Class
  1. Open Attendance from the school navigation menu.
  2. The available classes will be displayed as cards.
  3. Locate the class whose attendance you want to record.
  4. Select Mark Attendance.

Management users can select active classes within their school. Class teachers can access the class assigned to them.

Selecting the Attendance Date
  1. On the Mark Attendance page, locate the Attendance Date field.
  2. Select the date for which you want to record attendance.
  3. Select View Date to load attendance for that date.
Weekend restriction: Attendance cannot be marked on Saturdays or Sundays. The system displays a warning when a weekend date is selected.
Recording Student Attendance

The attendance table displays each active student's admission number, name and attendance status.

  1. Locate the first student on the attendance list.
  2. Select Present if the student is in school.
  3. Select Absent if the student is not in school.
  4. Repeat the process for the other students.
  5. Review the attendance summary displayed above the table.
  6. Select Save Attendance to record your entries.

You can also use Mark All Present as a shortcut when all students are present. Review the selections and still select Save Attendance to record them.

Understanding attendance statuses: Each student can be marked Present or Absent. Students for whom neither option is selected remain Not Marked. Unmarked students do not receive an attendance record for that date.
Understanding the Daily Attendance Summary

The Mark Attendance page displays four figures to help you monitor the class:

  • Total Students: Number of active students in the class.
  • Present: Number of students marked present.
  • Absent: Number of students marked absent.
  • Not Marked: Number of students without an attendance record for the selected date.

You can revisit a previous weekday using the date selector. Existing attendance entries will be displayed and can be updated before saving again.

Viewing Class Attendance Summary

The Class Attendance Summary provides a broader view of attendance records for students in a selected class. This report requires the Advanced Attendance feature.

  1. Open Class Attendance Summary.
  2. Select the class you want to review.
  3. Choose a starting date using the From field.
  4. Choose an ending date using the To field.
  5. Select View Summary.

When available, the date range defaults to the current academic term's start and end dates.

Understanding the Class Summary

After selecting a class, the summary displays:

  • Total Students: Number of active students in the class.
  • Students Marked: Number of students with at least one attendance record in the selected date range.
  • Overall Attendance: Percentage calculated from all present and absent records in the selected date range.
  • Present Records: Total number of recorded present entries.
  • Absent Records: Total number of recorded absent entries.

The student table also displays each student's admission number, name, number of days marked, days present and individual attendance percentage.

Class teacher access: Class teachers can view attendance summaries only for their assigned classes. Other authorised management users can access classes within their school.
Viewing Personal Attendance History

Students can view their own attendance history through the Student Portal, provided the school subscription includes the Student Portal feature.

  1. Log in using your student account.
  2. Open the Student Dashboard.
  3. Locate the attendance section.
  4. Select the attendance history option.

The My Attendance History page displays:

  • Current academic term, where available.
  • Days Present.
  • Days Absent.
  • Days Marked.
  • Attendance Rate.

A history table also shows each recorded attendance date, the day of the week and whether the student was marked Present or Absent.

Student attendance history: When the current academic term has both a start date and an end date, the history is limited to attendance records within that term. Students can only view their own attendance history.
Important: Attendance percentages are calculated from recorded attendance entries. Days without an attendance record are not counted as present or absent.

9. Timetable Management

The Timetable Management feature allows schools to organise lesson periods, configure subject requirements, generate weekly timetables and publish them for teachers and students.

Feature requirement: Timetable management requires the TIMETABLE feature to be enabled for your school.
Creating a Timetable
  1. Open the Timetable Management section.
  2. Select Create Timetable.
  3. Enter a Timetable Name.
  4. Select the Academic Session.
  5. Select the Academic Term belonging to that session.
  6. Choose the Days Used for the timetable.
  7. Select Create Timetable to save.

Newly created timetables begin with Draft status. You can later use the Configure option on the timetable list to change its name or selected days.

Creating Period Templates

Period templates allow schools to prepare reusable arrangements of school days and lesson periods.

  1. Open Period Templates.
  2. Enter a template name.
  3. Optionally provide a description.
  4. Set whether the template is the default template and whether it is active.
  5. Select Create Template.
  6. Open Configure beside the template.
  7. Use Add School Day to add the required days.
  8. Use the Add Block form under each day to configure its periods and breaks.

Configured blocks can be edited or deleted from the template configuration page.

Configuring Timetable Periods

Open Periods beside the timetable you want to configure.

You can either apply an existing active period template or add periods individually.

The Add Period form includes:

  • Period Name: Name of the lesson period or break.
  • Day: The school day.
  • Period Number: Position of the period in the day's timetable.
  • Start Time and End Time: The period's scheduled time.
  • Break Period: Indicates whether the period is a break.
  • Active Period: Determines whether the period is active.

Existing periods are displayed in a table where authorised users can edit or delete them.

Configuring Timetable Requirements

Requirements determine how frequently each subject allocation should appear in the weekly timetable.

  1. Open the timetable's Requirements page.
  2. Under Add Requirement, select an existing subject allocation.
  3. Enter the number of Lessons per week.
  4. Enter the number of Double periods per week.
  5. Select Add Requirement.

The allocation must belong to the timetable's school and academic term. Double periods cannot exceed half the number of weekly lessons.

The Configured Requirements table displays the class, subject, teacher, lessons per week and double periods per week. Existing requirements can be edited or deleted.

Preparing Requirements Automatically

Instead of adding every requirement manually, you can use Prepare / Sync Requirements.

This prepares missing timetable requirements from the existing subject allocations for the timetable. Existing requirements are preserved.

Generating the Timetable
  1. Confirm that the timetable periods are configured.
  2. Confirm that the required subject allocations and weekly lesson requirements are available.
  3. Open the Requirements page.
  4. Select Generate Timetable.

The system attempts to generate lesson entries using the configured timetable periods and requirements. A success message displays the number of lesson entries generated. If generation encounters a problem, an error message explains the issue.

Viewing and Approving a Timetable

Select View Timetable to inspect the generated timetable.

The timetable is displayed as a weekly grid containing days and periods. Each lesson entry shows the class, subject and assigned teacher. Break periods are also identified.

You can filter the timetable by school section and class.

The timetable follows these approval stages:

  1. Draft: The initial status of a newly created timetable.
  2. Approved: An authorised management user can approve a Draft timetable after lesson entries have been generated.
  3. Published: An authorised management user can publish an Approved timetable.
Important: A timetable must contain generated lesson entries before it can be approved. Only Approved timetables can be published.
Viewing the Timetable as a Teacher
  1. Open your Teacher Dashboard.
  2. Locate My School Timetable.
  3. Select View Full Timetable.

The teacher timetable displays the published timetable for the current academic term, including the scheduled classes, subjects and assigned teachers.

Viewing the Timetable as a Student
  1. Log in using your student account.
  2. Open the Student Dashboard.
  3. Locate My Timetable.
  4. Select View Full Timetable.

Students can view the published timetable for their own class. The timetable displays the subjects, assigned teachers, days and lesson periods.

Student access: The school must have both the TIMETABLE and STUDENT_PORTAL features enabled for students to access their timetable.
Remember: Teacher and student timetable pages display the published timetable for the current academic term. If no current term or published timetable is available, the timetable cannot be displayed.
Access and permissions: Score entry, review, approval, publication and report-card editing are controlled by school permissions. Users may see different options depending on their assigned responsibilities. All these operations are restricted to the user's school. Contact your school administrator if you cannot access an expected feature.
